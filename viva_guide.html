📐 1. Core Mathematical Formulations

Exam Must-Know

A. Appliance Energy Consumption (kWh)

\[ E = \frac{P \times h \times d \times Q}{1000} \text{ kWh} \]

Where: P = Rated Power in Watts (W), h = Average operating hours/day, d = Days in month (typically 30), Q = Appliance quantity. We divide by 1,000 because electrical utility meters record energy in Kilowatt-hours (kWh), where \(1\text{ kW} = 1000\text{ W}\).

Worked Example: A 75 W ceiling fan used 8 hours/day for 30 days:
\[ E = \frac{75 \times 8 \times 30}{1000} = 18\text{ kWh} \]

B. Total Monthly Household Consumption

\[ E_{\text{total}} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} E_i \]

Summation of the individual monthly energy consumption \(E_i\) across all \(n\) registered household or hostel appliances.

Daily Average: \(E_{\text{daily}} = \frac{E_{\text{total}}}{30}\) units/day.

C. Tiered Slab Bill Calculation

\[ \text{Energy Charge} = \sum_{k=1}^{m} (U_k \times R_k) \] \[ \text{Total Bill} = (\text{Energy Charge} + \text{FC}) \times \left(1 + \frac{\text{Duty}}{100}\right) \]

Where \(U_k\) represents units falling into tariff slab \(k\), and \(R_k\) is the unit rate for that slab. FC is fixed charge and Duty is government electricity duty tax.

D. Energy Savings & Carbon Footprint

\[ \Delta E = \frac{P \times (h_{\text{old}} - h_{\text{new}}) \times d \times Q}{1000} \] \[ \text{Monthly Savings} = \Delta E \times \text{Rate} \] \[ \text{CO}_2 \text{ Cut} = \Delta E \times 0.82\text{ kg CO}_2\text{e / kWh} \]

Quantifies direct rupee savings and greenhouse gas mitigation from lifestyle adjustments or equipment upgrades.

🎓 2. Top Viva Voce Questions & Model Answers

Viva Preparation
Q1: Why do we divide by 1,000 in the appliance energy formula?
Answer: Appliance power ratings are stated in Watts (W), which is Joules per second. Electricity utility meters measure consumption in Kilowatt-hours (kWh), commonly referred to as "units". Because 1 Kilowatt equals 1,000 Watts (\(1\text{ kW} = 1000\text{ W}\)), multiplying Watts by hours gives Watt-hours (Wh). Dividing by 1,000 converts Watt-hours into Kilowatt-hours (kWh).
Q2: Why does your project not require expensive IoT sensors or smart meters?
Answer: IoT hardware solutions (smart plugs, current transformers, microcontrollers) are cost-prohibitive for ordinary households and students (costing ₹2,000–₹5,000 per socket). Our software-based analytical approach relies on known manufacturer wattage specifications, standardized duty cycles, and user usage patterns. This achieves ~90-95% estimation accuracy for a fraction of the cost, making energy audits accessible to everyone instantly via any web browser.
Q3: What is "Duty Cycle" and why is it important for refrigerators and air conditioners?
Answer: Appliances with compressors do not run at full rated power continuously. A refrigerator is plugged in 24 hours a day, but its compressor cycles ON and OFF to maintain temperature. The duty cycle is the percentage of time the compressor is active (typically 35%–40% in standard ambient conditions). If a 180W refrigerator were assumed to consume 180W for 24 hours continuously, the estimate would be 2.5× higher than reality. Our calculator adjusts continuous compressor appliances by their duty cycle.
Q4: What is "Phantom Power" or "Standby Power Drain"?
Answer: Phantom load (or vampire power) is electricity consumed by electronic devices while they are turned off or in standby mode (e.g. televisions waiting for remote signals, microwave digital clocks, laptop chargers left plugged in). While each draws only 1W to 10W, over 24 hours across multiple appliances it can account for 5% to 10% of a home's total electricity bill. Our system explicitly tracks and highlights standby drain.
Q5: How does a Tiered Slab Tariff prevent excessive energy consumption?
Answer: Under progressive tiered pricing (slab tariff), the initial lifeline units (e.g., 0–100 kWh) are subsidized and cheap (e.g., ₹3.75/kWh). As consumption increases into higher tiers (e.g., > 300 or > 500 kWh), the incremental rate increases steeply (e.g., ₹11.20/kWh). This creates a strong financial incentive for consumers to conserve energy to remain in lower billing tiers.
Q6: What is a BLDC fan and why does it save so much energy over induction fans?
Answer: A conventional ceiling fan uses a single-phase AC induction motor consuming 75W to 80W with high rotor copper losses. A Brushless DC (BLDC) fan uses permanent magnets and an electronic inverter circuit to commutate DC power. It produces the same airflow consuming only 28W to 32W—saving ~65% energy with less heat generation and higher motor efficiency.
Q7: What database and software architecture was chosen for this project?
Answer: The application follows the Model-View-Controller (MVC) architectural pattern. The backend is written in Python (Flask) for mathematical calculation and API routing. The database is a relational SQLite database with tables for users, appliances, tariffs, and historical bill records. The frontend utilizes HTML5, custom responsive CSS, vanilla JavaScript, Chart.js for data visualization, and ReportLab for programmatic PDF audit report generation.
